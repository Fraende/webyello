## Design Specificity Verdict

**LLM assessment:** Authored specifically for WebYello, not a generic small-business template. The hero's animation doesn't just decorate — the yellow and blue blobs converge via `mix-blend-mode:multiply` into olive at the exact moment the headline's ink-stamp reveal lands, dramatizing DESIGN.md's own "two ink drums misregistering" concept. The two-ink discipline is mechanically enforced (shadow cycling yellow→blue→olive, blue care-plan strip, yellow featured plan), copy has real voice ("we pop the confetti," a bakery placeholder that "smells as good as our bread"), and the site turns its own newness into a stated trust move ("no imaginary portfolio, no invented reviews") rather than hiding it. The caveat: the information architecture (hero → 6 services → 4-step process → 3 tiers → contact) is a completely standard agency-site skeleton — specificity lives in surface (color, motion, type, voice), not structure. Legitimate for the genre, but worth naming.

**Deterministic scan:** `impeccable detect --json index.html` exits 2 with 19 findings: 1 warning (`clipped-overflow-container` — "body clips a positioned child"), 17 advisory (`design-system-font-size` — a long tail of one-off sizes like `.74rem`, `.88rem`, `1.9rem` not on DESIGN.md's documented ramp), 1 advisory (`em-dash-overuse`, 13 instances). The clipped-overflow warning has recurred across every prior audit/polish pass on this file without a reproducible visual bug on manual inspection — treating it as a known, still-unresolved false-positive candidate rather than a fresh issue. The font-size advisories are real but mostly legitimate one-off contextual sizes (badge, form labels, price displays) rather than systemic drift; not worth bloating DESIGN.md's ramp to silence them per its own "don't extract every token" guidance. No new warnings since the last pass.

**Visual overlays:** Not attempted this run (no live-server injection); findings above come from direct screenshot inspection and computed-style measurement instead.

**Correcting an LLM assumption with measured evidence:** the design review's accessibility persona (Sam) flagged the featured pricing plan's `.for` subtitle (opacity .75 on yellow) as a likely contrast problem. And the focus-indicator concern is real but narrower than first framed: form *inputs* genuinely rely on a color-only border swap (no outline), but links and buttons were verified via real keyboard Tab to get a proper native `:focus-visible` ring — the gap is scoped to form fields, not the whole page.

## Overall Impression

The site's best 3 seconds (the hero) are more thoughtfully crafted than most production landing pages ever get — motion that proves a design concept instead of riding on top of it. But craft budget clearly ran out before reaching the one screen state that actually matters commercially: the contact form's success state is a bare one-line swap in a box that stays visually intact for the (now-empty) form beside it, and the mailto link — the fastest alternative path to contact — reads as static text. The biggest opportunity isn't more delight; it's carrying the same authorship into the moments right before and after someone tries to become a lead.

## What's Working

1. **The hero animation embodies the metaphor, not just decorates it.** Two ink blobs converge from offset positions into an overlap that produces the documented "olive from yellow+blue misregistration," synced to a clip-path "stamp" landing on the headline's highlighted word. Rare: motion that proves the design system's own stated concept.
2. **A mechanically disciplined two-ink budget.** Every accent color on the page is yellow, blue, or their olive overlap — verified across ticket shadows, the care-plan strip, and the featured plan. This restraint is what keeps a loud, tilted, hard-shadowed print aesthetic from tipping into chaos.
3. **The honesty positioning is well-placed and structurally backed.** "No imaginary portfolio, no invented reviews" sits right before the pricing cards — pre-empting exactly the skepticism a price-conscious visitor would feel — and there's no testimonial/logo section anywhere on the page to contradict it.

## Priority Issues

**[P1] Contact form success state undercuts the site's best moment with its worst-designed one**
- **Why it matters:** This is the last screen state a converting visitor sees. The peak-end rule says it should be at least as considered as the hero was delightful; instead the two-column contact layout stays fixed while the form is swapped for one `<h3>`, leaving a large empty void beside unchanged copy.
- **Fix:** Keep the box's visual weight (padding/border/shadow) intact on success, and give the thank-you state concrete next-step detail instead of a single line.
- **Suggested command:** `/impeccable polish`

**[P1] The contact email has no link affordance**
- **Why it matters:** Measured: `hello@webyello.com` is byte-identical in color, weight, and decoration to the plain-text list items beside it (contrast is fine at 17.4:1 — this isn't a contrast problem). A visitor scanning for the fastest contact path can miss that it's clickable entirely.
- **Fix:** Give `.contact-list a` a distinct treatment (underline and/or brand-accent color) from its non-interactive siblings.
- **Suggested command:** `/impeccable polish`

**[P2] The hero's signature animation is invisible on mobile**
- **Why it matters:** `.hero-art{display:none}` below 900px removes the entire yellow/blue duotone illustration — the single most distinctive, effort-invested visual moment on the site never renders for a phone visitor, who instead meets "The Riso Print Desk" identity only much later via card shadow colors. For a local Finnish small-business audience, mobile is plausibly the majority of first visits.
- **Fix:** Design a lighter-weight mobile version of the registration animation (even a static two-circle overlap graphic would preserve the concept) rather than removing it outright.
- **Suggested command:** `/impeccable adapt`

**[P2] Empty project-description field is accepted, despite the "no back-and-forth" promise**
- **Why it matters:** Confirmed live: submitting with name+email filled and the message textarea empty succeeds with no validation. WebYello's stated differentiator is fixed-quote pricing with no back-and-forth — but the form as built can produce a near-content-free lead that requires exactly the follow-up the brand promises to avoid.
- **Fix:** Make the message field required, or add light validation encouraging real detail before submit.
- **Suggested command:** `/impeccable harden`

**[P2] Native browser validation breaks the Finnish-first experience at the highest-stakes moment**
- **Why it matters:** An empty required field surfaces the browser's own validation bubble in the browser's UI locale, not the page's own `I18N` Finnish copy — the one moment WebYello most wants to feel competently bilingual instead leaks an unstyled, possibly-English system message.
- **Fix:** Intercept validation with custom JS using the existing `I18N` dictionary for the error text.
- **Suggested command:** `/impeccable harden`

**Sam (Accessibility-Dependent User)**
- Confirmed: form inputs rely on a color-only focus signal (border-color swap from ink-black to blue, `outline:none`) — no shape change, weaker than a standard focus ring. (Links and buttons are fine — verified via real keyboard Tab to produce a native `:focus-visible` ring; the gap is scoped to inputs specifically.)

## Minor Observations

- All three pricing CTAs read identically ("Ask for a quote"/"Pyydä tarjous") — no copy nudge differentiating the featured "Business" tier's own button.
- The footer sign-off "Made with plenty of yellow" is wrapped in `<small>`, giving the brand's own closing line the lowest text hierarchy on the page.
- `<html lang="en">` is hardcoded in the static markup even though JS immediately flips it to `fi` — a negligible race for anything that reads `lang` before the script runs (crawlers, some assistive tech on a slow connection).
- Services grid renders all 6 cards in one ungrouped chunk, slightly over the ≤4-per-group scanning guideline; low-impact given clear card boundaries, but a candidate for pacing into two visual rows.
- The `✂` scissor glyph as a pricing bullet is a nice ticket-motif detail but is glyph-rendering-dependent across OS/browser combinations.

## Questions to Consider

1. If mobile is plausibly the majority of local Finnish small-business search traffic, and the hero's duotone animation is switched off entirely below 900px, is "The Riso Print Desk" actually the site's identity for most visitors — or a desktop-only flourish?
2. The hero got a custom two-stage animation; the contact form's success state — the page's only real conversion event — got a one-line `innerHTML` swap. If craft budget is finite, why does the moment right before a visitor leaves get none of it?
3. "No imaginary portfolio, no invented reviews" is the site's strongest trust move, stated once in a paragraph competing with three price cards. What would it look like to make that honesty a structural feature of the page rather than a single sentence?
